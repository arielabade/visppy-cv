<p align="center"><img src="assets/brand/visppy-wordmark.png" alt="Visppy logo" width="360"></p>

crowds, but not where attention accumulates, which paths connect areas, or where people disengage.

## 02 — Problem

- Where does attention or congestion accumulate?
- Which paths connect areas of the space?
- Where do people stay, pass through, or disengage?
- How should layout, staffing, signage or measurement change?

| Deliverable | Client question it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Zone map and heatmap | Where is visible demand concentrated? |
| Flow and journey view | How do people move between areas? |
| Occupancy and dwell view | Which areas retain attention or create pressure? |
| Time-based analysis | When should the team act? |
| Impact-versus-effort view | Which improvement should be tested first? |
| Measurement roadmap | What signal is still needed to connect behaviour to business results? |

Every hotspot is read together with the layout: it can be a meaningful area, a corridor, furniture or a
waiting point. Measurement quality is read per zone, because camera distance, occlusion and geometry
vary.

## 04 — Result

Three case studies, each ending in a decision:

| Case | Finding | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mandala** (stand) | A circulation-heavy side, a transition toward the central screen, and a consultative area. | Test a clearer call to action and staffing at the transition, without blocking the corridor. Separate passage from qualified attention. |
| **Loja** (store) | Outside flow, gateways, retention areas and fixed structures, mapped on the real layout instead of a generic three-zone story. | Measure the entrance and qualified permanence before claiming conversion. |
| **Palestra** (lecture room) | A mostly stationary audience is expected behaviour, not disengagement. | Use room-specific signals and explicit participation events, not stillness, to infer attention. |

<p align="center"><img src="assets/case-study/impact-effort-matrix.png" width="80%" alt="Impact versus effort matrix"></p>

> **Outcome.** Observations translate into an ordered list of experiments. The busiest area is not
> automatically the most valuable one.

## 05 — Limits and next move

Video shows visible behaviour and spatial patterns. **It does not prove** unique visitors, identity,
gaze, emotion, demographics, attention, conversion, sales or return on investment. Those need
supporting signals: validated entry lines, check-in, QR events, customer records, point-of-sale events,
human review or approved operational context.

The public portfolio excludes customer footage, identifiable people, private endpoints, credentials,
raw observation files, model weights and proprietary rules
([privacy policy](docs/privacy.md)).

**Next move:** connect zone-level dwell to point-of-sale events, so qualified attention can be tested
against revenue.
